### A2 as fixed

Run against the previous commit, the reported case was the mild one. A
global level beside one offset per instrument (exactly collinear) under a
Gaussian prior returned a finite **wrong** value: 240 log-units off on the
kernel, 8 on the MLX path and 3.7 in float64. Three changes:

- **The precision form.** The Gaussian-prior marginal is `N(r; Phi mu,
  K + Phi S Phi^T)` formed through `M = C + S^-1`, expanded about the
  centring fit as `quad = a - b^T M^-1 b - 2 b^T w + w^T C m`, `w = M^-1
  S^-1 m`, so no term cancels against another whether the prior is broad
  or tight. `S^-1` and `log|S|` are computed once in float64, and `S` is
  checked to be a symmetric positive-definite `(p, p)` at construction.
  `_marginal` returns the posterior directly, so `linear_posterior` no
  longer inverts `C` on the host. (A gradient in `v` is unchanged in form.)
- **A ridged centring fit.** The white-noise fit `beta0` inverted
  `Phi^T W Phi`, which is singular for such a basis (NaN, hence the old
  failure value for a zero column). The marginal is exactly invariant to
  `beta0`, so it is now solved on the unit-diagonal normal equations plus
  a ridge (1e-7 float32, 1e-15 float64); a zero column is centred at 0 with
  scale 1.
- **A structural collinearity refusal.** The old code refused a singular
  basis under the flat prior only *by accident*, through that NaN. With the
  fit fixed, an exactly collinear basis inverted in float32 rounding to a
  finite, meaningless value. A floor on the K-weighted system was tried
  and rejected: float32 polarisation of exactly parallel columns leaves a
  smallest eigenvalue of 2.8e-4, a float32 LU of `M` mis-reports its own
  determinant (the level + offsets case was 4.6 log-units off on the
  kernel and *not* caught), and a GP makes `C` ill-conditioned for good
  bases (two offsets with no level: kernel accurate to 6e-5 at a
  condition number the floor refused). So the basis is tested by its own
  structure, the unit-diagonal white-noise normal equations' log-det, which
  the centring fit already computes: below 1e-5 (float32) or 1e-12
  (float64) it is refused under the flat prior (improper), and in float32
  under the Gaussian one (float32 cannot resolve the degenerate direction;
  float64 is exact and is not refused).

Measured after (SHO, N = 300 and 500, against the dense marginal): broad
prior float32 1e-5 (kernel) / 1e-5 (MLX), float64 5e-13; zero column under
a Gaussian prior 2e-5 / 3e-5 / 6e-13 (was the failure value); level +
offsets float64 1e-12 (was 3.7 off), float32 refused (was 240 off); tight
prior far from the data 5e-4 / 1.3e-3 on a value of 3118 (5e-4 / 4.6e-3
before); two offsets, no level, flat 6e-5 / 3e-4. Posteriors 1e-13 to
1e-16 of dense. `tests/test_marginal.py`: the singular-basis test on every
path with float32 gradients vs float64 differences, the bad-prior
refusals, the collinear refusal on both routes and in float64.

### A5 as fixed

A basis returned as `(N, p)` -- shared by every chain, a constant -- is
evaluated **once**, at construction, in float64 from the float64
abscissa, and replaced by an orthonormal basis `Q = Phi A` (`Q^T W Q = I`,
`W = 1 / yerr^2`) that every evaluation uses
(`GPLogLike._cache_shared_basis`). Exact: the marginal is the same model
in `gamma = A^-1 beta`, the Gaussian prior mapped alike, and under the flat
prior `log|det A|` added back. `linear_posterior` maps the posterior back
to the user's `beta`; `predict` smooths `Q` and uses the posterior in
`gamma`, which is the same prediction.

`Q` comes from Gram-Schmidt, reorthogonalised, on the weighted
column-normalised basis. Two routes were tried and rejected on the way:
an SVD declared a BJD slope column dead (its singular value is 3.3e-14
of the largest, the 2.5e6 scale swamping it, where Gram-Schmidt's `R_jj`
measures 8e-8), and Householder QR gives a zero column an arbitrary
direction that later columns project on, so zeroing it afterwards broke
`Q = Phi A` (the zero-column test, 2.9 log-units in float64). A dead
direction is an exactly zero column: refused under the flat prior,
carried by the prior under a Gaussian one, scaled so its prior precision
matches the residual's size in the p x p system.

Why only a shared basis: the basis function receives `x` in float32 on the
GPU path, and float32 rounds a BJD to 0.25 d before the library sees it,
so a per-chain raw-time basis (recomputed every evaluation) cannot be
rescued; it keeps A2's refusal, and `mean_fn` has the same requirement
(pass times relative to a reference, anvil's float32 rule).

Kernel float32 against float64, before -> after (N = 4000, SHO; the
probe of E5/E6):

| basis | value | gradient |
|---|---|---|
| centred, shared | 1.5e-2 -> 2.7e-4 | 9.6e-6 -> 1.9e-6 |
| raw t (~3000), shared | refused (pre-A2 0.16) -> 2.7e-4 | refused -> 1.9e-6 |
| 3 columns, shared | 1.6e-2 -> 9.2e-4 | 8.9e-6 -> 2.2e-6 |
| centred, per chain | 1.5e-2 (unchanged) | 9.6e-6 (unchanged) |

So orthonormalising bought ~50x in the value for every shared basis, not
only raw times. Speed unchanged within noise (marginal_cost.py, quiet:
shared v+g 1.5 / 1.7 / 2.1 / 5.6x at p = 1 / 2 / 5 / 10). Tests: raw `[1, t]`
at t ~ 3000 and at a BJD, flat and Gaussian, every path, gradients and
the posterior in raw units, against the centred model (a raw-intercept
Gaussian prior at a BJD has no float64 reference and is skipped); the
level + offsets case under a Gaussian prior is now evaluated in float32
(within 1e-2) rather than refused; shared vs per-chain each within its
route's floor and equal in float64 to 1e-10.

Rejected for accuracy: replaying the Gram from zero entry means (the
cancellation the boundary plan removed); fusing the r-sensitivity pass into
the forward (value-only callers would pay); dropping the Joseph form.
